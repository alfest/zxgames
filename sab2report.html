Saboteur II, mission 8: completability check

Work log, last updated 2026-11-20

Result: mission 8 can be completed under these rules. It was completed on 15 November at the real timer 551 with 27 timer units to spare, 14 tapes, no invincibility and no cheats; the recording solver/work/rzx/mission8_chain11lift.rzx plays to the congratulations screen in Fuse 1.10. The sections below keep the work log in order, including the earlier conclusion (10 November) that an honest run was very likely impossible, which the final run disproved.

Task

Check whether mission 8 (ZAI, code "DIM MAK") of the Saboteur II mod can be completed under the conditions below, and confirm the result with an RZX recording of the run.

  • Collect 14 punched tapes, one from each of the 14 tape stashes. Stash (5,19) holds two tapes; only one is taken.

  • Do not use the invincibility room (4,13): entering it switches invincibility on at once, so it is excluded from the route.

  • No cheats and no code changes: the repository's saboteur2.tap build as is, Kempston joystick input.

  • Finish the mission: insert the tapes into the rocket console and escape on the motorbike before the timer runs out.

The question is twofold: does such a route exist, and which one is the fastest.

How the work is organised

The check goes from plan to practice: first a cheap route calculation on the map, then playing the real game with enemies.

  1. A ZX Spectrum 48K emulator with RZX recording, in sync with Fuse.

  2. Source analysis: targets on the map and the rules that affect the route.

  3. A movement model on the map tiles, checked against the emulator; distances between targets and the best visiting order.

  4. Playing the game leg by leg with automatic search in the emulator.

  5. Building the RZX from power-on and checking it in Fuse.

The tools are in the solver/ directory of the solver branch.

1. Emulator and RZX recording

A custom Z80/ZX Spectrum 48K emulator was written in C and is driven from Python. RZX files it records play back in Fuse 1.10 without desync: Fuse's screen frames and the emulator's matched to the end of the test recording.

  • Frame boundaries and the instruction counter work the same way as in Fuse's RZX playback (checked against its source).

  • The game loads from saboteur2.tap with the standard loader; in the menu the code "DIM MAK" is entered and Kempston is selected.

  • The game reads the joystick once per game frame, so a run is a sequence of Kempston values, one per frame.

  • The emulator saves and restores its state in ~12 µs and plays about 2500 game frames per second; this is the basis of the search.

  • The final recording starts with a snapshot right after loading and contains all input: menu and game.

All route points and the rules that set the route's cost were found in the source. Coordinates are rooms (x,y) on the 32×32 map.

The rules that matter most for the route:

  • Timer: 551 "seconds" of 20 game frames each, i.e. 11,020 frames for the whole mission; game speed does not affect it.

  • Health: 112 points, regenerates only while standing (and on a lift), 1 point per 2 frames. Damage taken is eventually paid for in time.

  • Fall damage: under 15 tiles 0, from 15 to 36 tiles half the height, from 37 tiles the full height.

  • The glider flies left; there are two sensible drop points: a jump on the first frame or the automatic drop at the end.

  • There are 101 enemies on the map, androids (some with flamethrowers) and pumas, each with its own patrol zone; bats also appear in several rooms.

3. Map model and visiting order

The visiting order was chosen by the minimum of "time + 2 frames per point of damage", i.e. including the time to regenerate health:

start → (17,7) → (15,8) → (8,2) → (4,10) → (5,19) → (13,24) → (12,16) → (17,19) → (1,30) → (22,29) → (24,28) → (30,21) → (27,7) → (22,7) → console (26,8) → motorbike (7,27). Rooms are given as (column, row); this is the order of the final run (stashes 25, 24, 05, 10, 12, 14, 18, 22, 13, 27, 28, 29, 16, 31).

How the distances were obtained:

  1. Tile model. All rooms were joined into one tile map; moves (step, small step, fall, jump, ladder, rope) are modelled from the game code. Shortest paths on it take seconds.

  2. Emulator check. For comparison, a full frame-by-frame search of moves (BFS) was run in a copy of the game without enemies. The model differs from it by 5% at the median (range 0.89–1.40).

  3. Distance matrix between the start, the 14 stashes, the console and the motorbike: 69 pairs exact (emulator), the rest from the model.

  4. Order: exact search over all orders of the 14 stashes by dynamic programming; the console and the motorbike always come last.

Route notes: stash (17,7) is taken first: the heroine drops from the glider on the very first frame, which costs no health, and the leg goes on with a jump off the tightrope. The way from the console to the motorbike across the whole map is the longest leg: on foot alone it is about 1500 frames, and the final run took 1,286 by riding the lift down from the top, which also heals (the ride itself is 163 frames). Room 9Eh has a moving platform: the model treats it as fixed, the emulator handles its exact behaviour.

4. Playing with enemies

The route is played in the real game leg by leg, from target to target. On the test leg (17,7) → (27,7) the ninja arrived in 531 frames versus 510 without enemies and lost 9 health points.

  • On every frame the 40 best variants are kept; each tries 11 joystick actions (stand, walk, jump, hit).

  • A variant scores higher the closer the ninja is to the target by the map model, the less damage taken and the more the enemies are hurt. Variants with too little health or entering the invincibility room are dropped.

  • Where the model does not know the way (the glider drop, the entrance to the right building towards (22,7) and the console), a path found by search in the enemy-free copy of the game serves as a hint.

  • At a stash the ninja takes items until one tape is obtained, at the console she inserts the tapes, and she mounts the motorbike with Up.

  • Success: the congratulation screen and the code for mission 9 given by the game.

What we learned along the way:

  • The drop at the end of the flight costs 47 energy: a fall from the sky through (9,1) and (9,2) down to (9,3), counter 47 tiles. A first-frame drop does no damage; the 101 damage on the first leg of the main run were two falls on a route chosen by the old search.

  • The enemy-free copy of the game does not behave like the real one, so all legs are searched breadth-first in the real game; the path found replays exactly.

  • The model does not see stash (17,7): the heroine walks to a spot in (18,4) and repeats the inputs of the earlier run (over the rope above (23,4): she must not stop or turn on it).

  • Resting on the roof up to 112 was a mistake; ~95 would have been enough: ~18 frames wasted.

  • Lifts were missing from the model, and the search towards the motorbike got stuck. Shafts (11,6)–(11,15), (15,16)–(15,25), (22,13)–(22,22) were added; the leg was played by waypoints: lift platform in (22,13), ride down, ladders to (7,27).

Conclusions:

  • Projectiles are 57% of the damage (861). This was the main reserve, and the tactics later added to the search and the rules of the final run were aimed at it: (1) shaking an enemy off by leaving the screen and coming back when near a border, otherwise strong hits (a weapon thrown from afar, a flying kick, the up kick, the crouch hit, the up-diagonal throw; pumas need a diagonal throw or a crouch kick; killed enemies do not respawn); (2) enemies that block a rest or a stash are dealt with before the tape is taken, and the weapon is never thrown when no enemy is in the room; (3) bats cannot be killed: dodge them by crouching, stopping or stepping back, never walk in step with one, never jump into one, and rest outside bat rooms; (4) the search key holds enemy, shot and bat positions, so waiting for them to pass is a real move; (5) a stash counts as reached only with hp 28 or more and no android or puma within 12 columns; (6) ladders and a flying kick instead of long drops, and a lift ride (it heals) with no rest before it; (7) no reward for hurting enemies that are not in the way.

  • Falls: 453. 148 of them before invincibility (the landing 47 and the jump off the roof 101) would also happen in an honest run; the 180 on the leg to (30,21) are falls possible only with invincibility, another way is needed there (one fall of ~59 is unavoidable).

  • The ~840 spare frames are the budget for rest (2 frames per point), fights and tricks together; healing in a lift costs no time.

  • For an honest run the search must choose not the fastest path but the cheapest by "frames + cost of damage", with tricks against enemies and lifts.

An honest run cannot follow the route of the invincible run at all. On the leg from the stash in (27,7) to the stash in (30,21) that route falls from a height that costs 180 energy, and the heroine has at most 112. A landing takes nothing under 15 tiles, half the counter from 15 to 36 tiles, and the whole counter from 37 tiles, so the honest run cannot take this leg as recorded: only one fall of about 59 on that way is unavoidable, the rest are drops that only invincibility survives. The recording came through because invincibility made every fall free. Resting, fighting better or a faster path do not help here: the order of the stashes has to change. The honest runs therefore take this part in the other direction: the stash in (30,21) comes before (27,7), and (27,7) is reached from below by the lift and a short drop onto it with a flying kick (8 hp in the final run).

Conclusion on the honest run (10 Nov; disproved by the run of 15 Nov)

On 10 November the mission looked very likely not completable within the timer under the rules of the task (the run of 15 November, described in the last sections, completed it): the best order found needs about 10,905 frames plus about 150 frames of tape taking, against 10,480 frames left on the timer at the first stash. The shortage is about 580 frames (29 timer seconds), and the real chained run cost a further 292 frames in its first four legs. This is a search result, not a mathematical proof: other visiting orders were not re-measured with the new search settings.
